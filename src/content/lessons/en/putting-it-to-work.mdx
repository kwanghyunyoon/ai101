You have the mental model and the habits. This Lesson is just practice. It walks
through five everyday tasks. Each one gives you a prompt to copy and a note on
what to check in the reply. Change the details to fit your own life.

## Reply to an awkward email

A coworker sent a short, cold email and you are not sure how to answer without
making it worse. Give the tool the gist and the tone you want, not the real
thread.

<TryIt
  prompt="Help me reply to a coworker who emailed asking, a bit sharply, why a report was late. It was late because I was waiting on numbers from another team. I want to sound calm and professional, not defensive, and offer a new date. Three or four sentences."
  lookFor="A short reply that owns the delay without over-apologizing, names the cause plainly, and gives a clear next date. If it sounds stiff, reply with 'warmer and shorter' and send that."
/>

Check that it did not invent a specific date or a detail you did not give.
Swap in the real date before you send.

## Plan something

You want to plan a weekend trip, a birthday, or just your week, and your
thoughts are scattered. Hand the tool the constraints and let it build the
first draft.

<TryIt
  prompt="Help me plan a two-day weekend trip to a coastal town for two adults who like walking, good coffee, and not rushing. We arrive Saturday late morning and leave Sunday evening. Give a loose schedule with a couple of options each half-day, not a packed itinerary."
  lookFor="A relaxed plan with room to move and a note where timing is tight. Treat any named cafe or hotel as a lead to look up yourself, not a booking."
/>

A good planning reply gives you a structure to react to. Push back on it: "less
driving," "add a rainy-day option," "we are on a tighter budget."

## Learn a new topic

You keep hearing a term and nodding along. Ask for a beginner explanation, then
ask follow-ups in the same chat until it clicks.

<TryIt
  prompt="Explain what a mortgage escrow account is, to someone who has never owned a home. Use plain words and one everyday example. Keep it to a short paragraph."
  lookFor="A clear paragraph with a concrete example and no jargon left undefined. If a part is still fuzzy, ask a plain follow-up in the same chat and let it build on its own answer."
/>

The value is in the follow-ups. Ask "what would you compare that to?" or "what
do people get wrong about this?" Keep going until you could explain it to
someone else.

## Summarize a long document

You have a long report, contract, or thread and 10 minutes. Ask for a summary
shaped the way you need it.

<TryIt
  prompt="Here is a long document. Summarize it in about 150 words for someone who has to decide whether to read the whole thing. Then list the three points that matter most and any dates or numbers I should not miss. [paste the text]"
  lookFor="A summary that matches what you skim in the source, a short list of real key points, and correct dates and figures. If a number looks off, check it against the document — the summary can misread or invent one."
/>

Always skim the original for anything the summary might have flattened. A
summary is a compression, and the tool can smooth over the one line that
mattered.

## Get unstuck by talking it through

You are stuck and cannot even phrase a clean question. That is fine. Describe
the mess and ask the tool to help you think.

<TryIt
  prompt="I need to tell a client that a project will slip by two weeks. I do not know how much detail to give, whether to offer something to make up for it, or how to word the opening. Help me think through what to decide, then we can draft it."
  lookFor="Questions back to you and a short list of decisions to make, not an instant finished email. Once you have answered those, ask it to draft the message."
/>

Talking a task through out loud is a real use, not a lesser one. The
back-and-forth is often where the next step shows up.

## Keep going

Every one of these used the same moves from Lesson 3. You give context. You say
the form you want. You steer the reply with a follow-up. Re-read Lesson 3 any
time a prompt is not landing.
