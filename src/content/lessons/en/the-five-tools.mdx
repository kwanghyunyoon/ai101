You have heard the names — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity — and maybe
wondered which one you are "supposed" to use. This Lesson gives you a short,
honest read on each, so you can pick one and get started.

## They are more alike than different

Four of these five are general assistants. You type a question or a request, and
they write back. They all handle the everyday jobs well: drafting an email,
planning a trip, explaining a topic, summarizing a page. The gaps between them
are real but small, and they shift often.

So do not agonize over the choice. The differences below are tendencies, not
hard rules. Perplexity is the one true exception, and we will come back to it.

This Lesson avoids version numbers and "which one scored higher" claims on
purpose. Those go stale in weeks. What each tool is *for* changes much more
slowly. Check the "last reviewed" date at the top — if it is old, treat the
specifics with care.

## ChatGPT — the default all-rounder

**Best at:** a bit of everything. It has the largest set of extra features. That
includes voice chat, image generation, image reading, and many add-ons built by
other companies.

**Free vs paid:** a capable free tier. The paid plan raises usage limits and
unlocks the newest features first.

**How to start:** go to chatgpt.com or install the app, make an account, and
type in the box.

**One standout:** you can talk to it out loud and it talks back, which makes it
easy to use while your hands are busy.

## Claude — long documents and careful writing

**Best at:** working with long documents, careful and natural-sounding writing,
and coding help. It tends to follow detailed instructions closely and to say
when it is unsure.

**Free vs paid:** a usable free tier. The paid plan gives more usage and handles
larger documents in one go.

**How to start:** go to claude.ai or install the app, make an account, and start
a chat.

**One standout:** you can paste in a very long report or a whole set of notes
and ask questions across all of it at once.

## Gemini — tied into Google's apps

**Best at:** being where you already work if you use Google. It connects to
Gmail, Google Docs, and Search, and it handles large amounts of text in one
prompt.

**Free vs paid:** a free tier tied to your Google account. The paid plan comes
bundled with extra Google storage and deeper access inside the Google apps.

**How to start:** go to gemini.google.com and sign in with a Google account, or
open it from the Google app.

**One standout:** when you allow it, it can act on your own Gmail and Docs. For
example: "find the flight confirmation in my inbox and add it to my calendar."

## Grok — built into X, tuned for right now

**Best at:** telling you what people are posting on X (formerly Twitter) at this
moment. It has a looser content filter than the others, so it pushes back less
on edgy or blunt requests.

**Free vs paid:** a limited free tier. Fuller access comes with an X
subscription or a paid Grok plan.

**How to start:** open it inside the X app or go to grok.com and make an account.

**One standout:** ask "what are people saying about this right now" and it pulls
from live posts, which the others do less well.

## Perplexity — the research one

Perplexity is not a general assistant. It is closer to a search engine that
writes you an answer. You ask a question and it searches the web. It replies with
the key points, **plus numbered links to the pages it used**.

**Best at:** looking things up when you need to trust and check the answer —
prices, dates, "is this still true," comparing products.

**Free vs paid:** a solid free tier. The paid plan adds deeper multi-step
research and higher limits.

**How to start:** go to perplexity.ai or install the app. You can ask a question
without an account, but making one saves your history.

**One standout:** every claim has a source you can click. When being able to
verify matters, this is the one to reach for.

## Pick one, stick with it for a week

You do not need all five. For almost everything you will want to do, one general
assistant is plenty. Add Perplexity when you have something to look up and
verify.

If you cannot decide, that is a sign the choice does not matter much yet. Pick
one and put it on your phone. Use it for a week on real tasks — an email, a plan,
a question you would have googled. You will learn more from that week than from
any comparison.

Next, you will look at how to use these tools safely and honestly — what not to
paste in, and when to say you used AI.
